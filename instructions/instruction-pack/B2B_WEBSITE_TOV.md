## 1 Core writing objective

Every page must help business customers understand the solution, compare the available choices and take the next relevant action. Content must combine practical value with transparent commercial information. The Agent must not trade clarity for creativity.

| Priority | Required outcome |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Understand | The product, offer and intended business need are immediately clear. |
| 2 Evaluate | Benefits, price, features and important conditions are easy to compare. |
| 3 Trust | Claims are specific, approved and supported by transparent terms. |
| 4 Act | The next step is clear through an accurate and supportive call to action. |

## 2 Voice profile

| Voice principle | Required behaviour | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear and solution-oriented | Lead with the business solution and practical outcome. Use direct verbs, simple words and concise recommendations. | Jargon, internal terminology, abstract promises and technical detail without explanation. |
| Reassuring and transparent | State price, eligibility, contract, promotional period, charges, customer rights and limitations clearly. | Hidden conditions, vague fees, selective detail and reassurance unsupported by facts. |
| Professional and approachable | Use confident, informative language with supportive phrasing where useful. | Slang, jokes, exaggerated friendliness, hype and pressure. |
| Structured and informative | Use headings, short paragraphs, lists, steps, cards, FAQs and clear comparison structures. | Dense text blocks, repetition, meandering explanations and mixed information types. |
| Commercially useful | Connect verified features to business value and lead to a relevant action. | Feature dumping, empty slogans and claims with no decision value. |

## 3 Mandatory perspective rules

These rules override wording found in older or reference pages.

| Rule | Application | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Never use I | No first-person singular in any component. | Avoid: I can help with the right plan. |
| Never use audience-facing you | Do not use you, your, yours or yourself to address the audience. | Avoid: Power your business with AI. |
| Use we, our and us | Use only when etisalat is clearly speaking, acting or supporting. | Our business plans combine connectivity and flexible options. |
| Prefer product-led neutral phrasing | Use the product, service or business outcome as the sentence subject when “we” would sound forced. | Business Pro AI combines high-speed internet and built-in AI. |
| Do not overuse we | Brand voice must not become self-centred. Keep the benefit or solution central. | Prefer: Flexible plans support business growth. |

Perspective conversion. “Power your business with next-generation connectivity” becomes “Power business growth with next-generation connectivity” or “Our next-generation connectivity supports faster, more efficient operations.”

## 4 Tone mechanics

## Sentence construction

Use active voice and concrete verbs.

Keep one main idea per sentence.

Lead with the solution or benefit, then add proof.

Use short paragraphs of two to four sentences.

Use imperatives only when they do not require audience-facing pronouns: “View plans,” “Choose a device,” “Contact Sales,” “Explore add-ons.”

Use friendly language selectively. Keep technical, pricing and contractual content factual.

Define specialist terms at first use when the audience may not know them.

## Punctuation and capitalisation

Use sentence case for page headings and body labels unless a UI design applies visual capitals.

Avoid semicolons. Split complex thoughts into shorter sentences.

Avoid dashes as sentence punctuation. Retain necessary compound terms such as high-speed, add-on and penalty-free.

Use exclamation marks rarely. Do not use them in plan facts, terms, FAQs or eligibility content.

Use official product names and capitalisation exactly as approved.

Use etisalat as the brand form. Use etisalat UAE when market context is required.

PROPOSED ADDITION  Use the approved Brand Dictionary as the terminology source of truth. Official product, service, plan and brand names must be reproduced exactly. Do not create alternative short forms, acronyms, spellings or capitalisation unless the Brand Dictionary explicitly permits them.

## 5 Message hierarchy

Business need or opportunity: state the relevant problem or goal.

Solution: explain what the product or service does.

Primary benefit: show the strongest business outcome.

Proof: add verified speed, allowance, coverage, price, feature, timing or support information.

Choice: organise plans or options for easy comparison.

Reassurance: explain eligibility, contract, charges, customer rights and support.

Action: provide one clear next step.

Do not include every detail from the brief in the main page flow. Prioritise information that affects the purchase decision. Move secondary detail into plan information, Important to Know or FAQs.

## 6 Benefit and feature writing

| Writing pattern | Formula | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solution led | Action or solution + business outcome | Stay connected across the UAE and KSA with one business plan. |
| Product led | Product name + capability + benefit | Business Pro AI combines high-speed internet with built-in AI tools. |
| Feature to benefit | Verified feature + what it enables | Carryforward data keeps unused allowance available for the following month. |
| Proof led | Outcome + measurable evidence | Support business growth with speeds of up to 1Gbps. |
| Reassurance led | Condition or right + clear effect | No exit charges apply during the five-month promotional period. |

Avoid stacking several benefits without explaining their relevance. Select the benefits that matter most to the target segment and connect each feature to a practical business outcome.

## 7 Component specific standards

## Hero banner

| Element | Purpose | Writing rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | Identify the product, offer or strongest outcome. | Keep it concise. Use the product name or a verified commercial hook. |
| Subtitle | Add the main differentiator or business value. | Complement the title. Do not repeat it. |
| Primary CTA | Move to the main conversion action. | Use a precise verb such as View plans, Buy now, Apply now or Contact Sales. |
| Offer label | Highlight a current commercial advantage. | Use only approved discounts, periods and conditions. |

Reference pattern: “Business Pro AI” / “More speed. AI ready. More business.” The pattern is concise and benefit-led. For formal content generation, the Agent may use a clearer supporting line such as “High-speed internet and AI solutions in one business plan.”

## Intro section

Use one descriptive heading and a short paragraph. Explain the solution, key business value and strongest proof. Avoid generic digital transformation language. A product introduction should normally answer what it is, what it enables and why it is different.

## USP cards

Use a two to five word title and one short supporting sentence. Keep all cards grammatically parallel. Three to five USPs are normally sufficient. Use verified labels such as Flexible subscription, Dedicated support or Easy digital management only when the supporting detail proves the claim.

## Plan and rate cards

Present decision-critical information in a consistent sequence across all plans.

| Order | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Plan name and promotional label |
| 2 | Monthly price and VAT status |
| 3 | Contract or promotional period |
| 4 | Primary allowances or speed |
| 5 | Distinctive benefit |
| 6 | Any material limitation |
| 7 | More information link |
| 8 | Conversion CTA |

Use identical labels and ordering across comparable cards. Do not mix promotional sentences into factual allowance lists. Where a feature varies by plan, show the value in the same row for every plan.

## Comparison tables

Name each comparison row in customer language.

Keep units and terminology consistent across columns.

Use “Included,” “Not included” or an exact value instead of unexplained symbols where space allows.

Place price, VAT and contract information near the top.

Do not hide major limitations below the table.

## Important to Know

Use this section for material commercial and operational information: eligibility, required documents, activation, billing, commitment, cancellation, promotional period, fair usage, device ownership, delivery and customer rights. Use short labelled statements or bullets. Do not use this section to conceal information that should appear beside the offer.

## FAQs

Write questions in natural customer language without using audience-facing “you.” Example: “When does the promotional period end?”

Answer directly in the first sentence.

Keep most answers between 25 and 70 words.

Repeat a key condition when it prevents misunderstanding.

Include only questions that remove a real purchase or service barrier.

## Support and contact banners

Use supportive, low-pressure language. State the situation, support available and next action. Example: “Still comparing the available plans? Our team can help identify the right business solution.” CTA: “Contact Sales.”

## Related content cards

Use a clear product or topic title, one practical benefit and a destination-specific CTA. Avoid vague CTAs such as Learn more when View devices, View plans or Explore roaming is more accurate.

## 8 Calls to action

| Intent | Preferred CTA | Avoid when a clearer action exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare | View plans, Compare plans | Discover more |
| Purchase | Buy now, Get this plan | Proceed |
| Application | Apply now | Submit |
| Lead | Contact Sales, Request a callback | Learn more |
| Product detail | View details, View features | Click here |
| Device | Choose a device, View devices | Explore |
| Support | Get support, View support options | Need help? |
| Download | Download application form | More info |

CTA labels should describe the destination or action. Avoid artificial urgency and pressure. Do not use “Start now” when the next step is only an information page or lead form.

## 9 Transparency and commercial information

| Information | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Price | State AED, billing frequency and whether VAT is included or excluded. |
| Starting price | Use “From” only when a lower entry option genuinely exists. |
| Discount | State the percentage, eligible plans and promotional period where relevant. |
| Free item | Confirm that no additional charge applies and state any qualifying condition. |
| Contract | State the commitment period and applicable exit condition. |
| Promotional period | Give the exact duration and clarify what happens afterwards. |
| Buyback value | Use “up to” where value varies and explain eligibility and assessment. |
| Customer right | State the right and qualifying condition in direct language. |
| Availability | Specify online-only, selected plans, selected customers or geographic limits. |
| Technical performance | Use “up to” and relevant conditions when performance can vary. |

Never invent a price, saving, speed, guarantee, eligibility rule, availability statement or customer right. If the brief is incomplete, the Agent must flag the missing fact rather than writing around it.

PROPOSED ADDITION  For time-sensitive facts such as price, offer period, allowance, eligibility, device specification, contract terms and legal wording, use the latest approved source and check any effective or expiry date provided. If two approved-looking sources conflict, do not choose one silently. Mark the issue as SOURCE CONFLICT and REQUIRED INPUT until the owning team confirms the correct fact.

Source precedence: current approved legal or regulatory material → current approved product/commercial source → approved Brand Dictionary → current approved website content → reference examples.

Older published content may guide structure or tone, but it must not override newer approved facts, pricing, terminology or legal conditions.

## 10 Content structure by page type

| Page type | Recommended sequence |
| --- | --- |
| Product detail | Hero → introduction → plans → benefits → process or eligibility → Important to Know → FAQs → related solutions |
| Offer page | Offer → value proposition → eligible plans → key benefits → conditions → FAQs → CTA |
| Category page | Category promise → product choices → differentiators → support → related solutions |
| Device page | Hero → device options → price and instalment → key features → eligibility → support → CTA |
| Solution page | Business need → solution → outcomes → capabilities → proof → lead CTA |
| Support page | Task statement → steps → requirements → completion state → related support |
| Homepage campaign | Commercial hook → key category shortcuts → priority launches → promotional content → support access |

## 11 SEO AEO and answer clarity

Use the primary customer search phrase naturally in the page title, H1, opening paragraph and one relevant subheading.

Use one clear H1 that identifies the product, offer or solution.

Write descriptive headings that retain meaning outside the page layout.

Give direct answers before supporting detail in FAQs and explanation sections.

Use complete product names, prices, units and eligibility terms so statements remain accurate when extracted.

Avoid vague pronouns and phrases such as this solution when the product name is needed for context.

Write descriptive link labels. Never use Click here.

Do not repeat keywords unnaturally or weaken the brand voice for search optimisation.

PROPOSED ADDITION  For GEO and generative-answer clarity, use exact product, service and brand entities in self-contained factual statements. Avoid vague pronouns where removing the product name could make an extracted answer ambiguous. Keep important prices, limits, eligibility conditions and qualifiers in the same sentence or immediate context as the claim they govern. Do not introduce unsupported statistics, awards, leadership claims or third-party authority signals for search visibility.

## 16 Agent workflow and validation

1.  Read the brief and separate verified facts from background information.

PROPOSED ADDITION  Before drafting, verify that decision-critical facts come from the latest approved source and that terminology matches the Brand Dictionary. Reference content must not be treated as current factual authority unless explicitly approved for that purpose.

2.  Identify the audience, page objective and primary conversion action.

3.  Prioritise the facts that most affect the customer decision.

4.  Map content to the approved components and their character limits.

5.  Draft the page in the required voice and perspective.

6.  Check that each feature is connected to a relevant business benefit.

7.  Check price, VAT, contract, eligibility, promotion and customer rights.

8.  Check structure, scannability, CTA accuracy, SEO and answer clarity.

9.  Count characters for every limited field and revise until compliant.

10.  Run the final prohibited-language and perspective scan.

11.  Return only copy that passes, with missing information clearly flagged.

PROPOSED ADDITION  If approved sources disagree on a material fact, stop final validation for the affected component. Preserve unaffected content where possible and return SOURCE CONFLICT / REQUIRED INPUT with the conflicting facts identified.
